# 004 — BYSETPOS is applied to a first period truncated at DTSTART

**Status:** Open, and materially narrower than the first version of this note.
Not novel — an equivalent report already exists upstream (`dateutil/dateutil`
[#1398], open since 2024-11-14, one comment). What is new here is a
primary-source citation, a mechanism with a per-case test, and honest limits.
Nothing has been sent upstream.

**Date:** 2026-09-05, revised the same day. **Affects:** `python-dateutil`
2.9.0.post0, `rrule.js` 2.8.1.

## The disagreement

```
DTSTART:20261231T090000            (Thursday)
RRULE:FREQ=WEEKLY;BYDAY=TU,TH,FR;BYSETPOS=2      (WKST defaults to MO)
```

The interval is the week 2026-12-28 (Mon) .. 2027-01-03 (Sun).
Instances generated by `BYDAY` in that interval: Tue 12-29, **Thu 12-31**,
Fri 01-01.

- `src/naive.py` applies `BYSETPOS=2` to that whole set → Thu **2026-12-31**,
  which is `DTSTART` itself.
- `python-dateutil` and `rrule.js` both return **2027-01-01** first, and never
  return 12-31.

## Mechanism, and how far it actually reaches

The claim: both implementations drop instances earlier than `DTSTART`
**before** applying `BYSETPOS`, so in `DTSTART`'s own period `BYSETPOS` indexes
into a truncated set; from the second period onward nothing is truncated.

`src/crosscheck.py` turns that into a per-case test rather than an assertion:
re-run `dateutil` with `DTSTART` moved back to the start of its own period — so
no truncation is possible — drop the results before the original `DTSTART`, and
check whether the divergence disappears.

| Cases | Result |
|---|---|
| 8 (all `FREQ=WEEKLY` with `BYSETPOS`) | explained: the substitution reproduces the full-period expansion exactly |
| 5 (all containing `BYWEEKNO`) | **not** explained |

Of the five: three have no `BYSETPOS` at all and first diverge 11–17 years
after `DTSTART`; one (`FREQ=YEARLY;INTERVAL=3;BYWEEKNO=52,-2;BYSETPOS=1`)
diverges only at the eighth occurrence, in 2050; one
(`FREQ=YEARLY;BYMONTH=1,5;BYWEEKNO=1,52;WKST=SU;BYSETPOS=2`) diverges at the
first occurrence but is still not repaired by the substitution. These are
plausibly the week-numbering question in finding 002; that is a hypothesis, not
a result, and they remain unadjudicated.

## Corrected reproduction

`dateutil` 2.9.0.post0, `DTSTART:20241110T090000` (Sunday),
`FREQ=WEEKLY;WKST=WE;BYDAY=MO,TU,WE;BYSETPOS=n`. The week containing `DTSTART`
runs Wed 06 Nov .. Tue 12 Nov, so the full-period candidate set is
**Wed 06, Mon 11, Tue 12 Nov**.

| BYSETPOS | dateutil | full-period reading |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mon 11 Nov | Wed 13 Nov (Wed 06 is selected, then filtered) |
| 2 | Tue 12 Nov | Mon 11 Nov |
| 3 | Tue 19 Nov | Tue 12 Nov |
| -1 | Tue 12 Nov | Tue 12 Nov (agrees) |

The earlier table gave 18 Nov and 19 Nov for positions 2 and 3 and marked
position 3 as agreement. Both were wrong.

The upstream reporter's own diagnosis in [#1398] — that the behaviour is about
`BYDAY` not being in "natural order" — is not supported: `BYDAY=SA,SU` is in
ascending `WKST=WE` order and still diverges (`BYSETPOS=1` → dateutil
Sun 10 Nov, full-period reading Sat 16 Nov, because Sat 09 Nov is selected then
filtered). Their contrasting `BYDAY=WE,TH,FR` example has every candidate
before `DTSTART`, so the first period is empty under both readings and nothing
can show.

## What RFC 5545 says

§3.3.10, on `BYSETPOS`:

> BYSETPOS operates on a set of recurrence instances in one interval of the
> recurrence rule.  For example, in a WEEKLY rule, the interval would be one
> week  A set of recurrence instances starts at the beginning of the interval
> defined by the FREQ rule part.

("one week  A set" — the missing period is in the published text.)

§3.8.5.3, on `RRULE`:

> The "DTSTART" property value SHOULD be synchronized with the recurrence
> rule, if specified.  The recurrence set generated with a "DTSTART" property
> value not synchronized with the recurrence rule is undefined.

Verified against the authoritative text, not a mirror:
`https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5545.txt`,
sha256 `c256f809479d98aa23d71bbd1658b3800ea9f13f41ca56e59c8d2de1b31cbfcb`,
retrieved 2026-09-05.

## Applicability: why this does not establish non-conformance

§3.8.5.3 makes the recurrence set undefined when `DTSTART` is not synchronized
with the rule. Whether it *is* synchronized here is **itself decided by the
reading under dispute**, and the two readings disagree in opposite directions
on the same rule:

| `FREQ=WEEKLY;BYDAY=TU,TH,FR;BYSETPOS=n`, DTSTART Thu 2026-12-31 | dateutil's first occurrence | full-period first occurrence |
|---|---|---|
| n=1 | **Thu 31 Dec** (= DTSTART → synchronized under dateutil's reading) | Tue 05 Jan (not synchronized) |
| n=2 | Fri 01 Jan (not synchronized) | **Thu 31 Dec** (= DTSTART → synchronized under the full-period reading) |

So neither side can use §3.8.5.3 to dismiss the other: each reading has a case
that is synchronized by its own lights and still diverges. That is a reason the
question is *open*, not a reason my reading wins. My preference for the
full-period reading is textual — §3.3.10 says where the set starts — and I
cannot show that the alternative reading is prohibited.

**Conclusion: a reproducible behavioural difference and a documented
interpretation dispute. Not a demonstrated specification violation.**

## Cross-implementation data (weak by construction)

Under matching bounds (eight occurrences requested from each, no horizon clip),
`rrule.js` 2.8.1 agrees with `python-dateutil` 2.9.0.post0 on **all 13**
disputed synchronized cases, and with `naive` on none. Per finding 003,
`rrule.js` documents itself as a port of `python-dateutil`, so this is close to
zero independent evidence — and the earlier suggestion that two divergences
hinted at ambiguity has been withdrawn, because those divergences were an
artifact of my own comparison. Data: `findings/data/004-crosscheck.json`.

## Assessment

Two widely deployed implementations, related by descent, share a behaviour that
a plain reading of §3.3.10 does not support, in a corner rare in practice. An
upstream report already exists and has sat for ~22 months.

Proportionate action is to document rather than accuse. Filing a duplicate of
[#1398] would add noise; adding the mechanism, the citation and the correction
of the reporter's diagnosis *to* [#1398] would add something, and that requires
[REQ-0004].
